The first dealer accredited by DJI to deliver official training in the Benelux is now up and running. It is Drone Parts Center, based in Nivelles, Belgium, whose operations manager, Stéphane Tilmant, completed DJI's GEO Trainer certification in August 2026. That credential lets the company offer the brand's official courses for the Matrice 4E mapping drone and the Zenmuse L3 LiDAR sensor, as part of the Advanced GEO Products Training Services programme DJI Enterprise launched in Europe in 2026.

The Benelux is the economic and political cooperation area made up of Belgium, the Netherlands and Luxembourg. DJI's pitch is not about flying, but about what happens before and after the flight: mission planning, data capture, RTK or PPK workflows, processing, quality control and delivering usable results. That is precisely where a survey falls apart even when the drone itself works fine.

## What is taught, and with which equipment

The Matrice 4E course focuses on advanced photogrammetry: Smart 3D Capture missions, corridor, area and oblique shots, close-range flights and D-RTK 3 base station deployment. Trainees also learn to process data in DJI Terra, edit models with DJI Modify and carry out GIS analysis. The manufacturer puts the aircraft's coverage at up to 2 square kilometres per flight, which ties the training to uses such as topographic surveys, construction monitoring, stockpile calculations, and roof and facade surveys.

The Zenmuse L3 course takes another route: LiDAR mapping. According to DJI, the sensor fires up to two million laser pulses per second and offers vertical accuracy of around 3 centimetres at 120 metres. The syllabus covers LiDAR fundamentals, acquisition planning, complex missions, RTK and PPK workflows, point cloud classification, editing and reconstruction. Those are skills that matter when the terrain makes classic photogrammetry hard: forests, embankments, quarries, power lines and railways.

## How the training works

The theory starts with self-study on DJI's training portal. The material is available in English, with a French version expected in January 2027. Then come one or two days of hands-on training, in Nivelles or at the customer's site, in French or English and organised privately for each organisation.

Before the official certificate there is a practical and theoretical assessment. Access to updated course material stays valid for five years. DJI also notes that no surveying background is required and that the course is not a substitute for a remote pilot licence. There is no need to buy the equipment first either, so a company can use the training to check whether DJI's mapping solutions fit its actual workflow.

## What changes for the mapping business

The goal readable in the background is commercial: making sure mapping systems — increasingly sophisticated — are actually used rather than left half-idle. Drone Parts Center sums it up from its own experience.

"We sell increasingly capable drones, and we see too many customers using only part of them because nobody taught them the method," says Didier Van Nes, founder of Drone Parts Center. "With this certification we can finally hand over the manufacturer's know-how, not just the hardware."

For anyone considering a survey drone for the first time, the value is not in the spec sheet but in the promise of a repeatable method. A DJI machine with [LiDAR capabilities](/en/news/drones/lidar-drone-bathymetry/) looks much the same on paper across two different operators; what separates a useful survey from a pile of unprocessed data is the process behind it. Official training attacks exactly that gap, though its real effect will show in how many of those courses end in delivered projects rather than just certificates.
