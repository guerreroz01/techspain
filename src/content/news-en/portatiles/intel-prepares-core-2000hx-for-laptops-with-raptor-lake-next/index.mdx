Intel would be preparing a high-performance laptop processor family under the **Core 2000HX** brand, according to a leak disseminated by user Golden Pig Upgrade on Weibo and picked up by VideoCardz and TechPowerUp. The chips would rely on the "Raptor Lake Next" architecture, the same plan Intel already handles for its desktop processors of the LGA-1700 socket, which now makes the jump to the mobile HX format.

![Intel Logo](./assets/intel-logo.png)

## What is known about the Core 2000HX

According to leaked information, the Core 2000HX would maintain a configuration of up to 8 performance cores (P-Cores) and 16 efficiency cores (E-Cores), that is, 24 cores in total, following the current Raptor Lake and Raptor Lake Refresh lines. The key is that they would reuse wafers manufactured on the Intel 7 node —the old "10 nm"— not a more recent lithography, with minor adjustments similar to those of the Refresh and focused mainly on improving D2D (between dies) latency.

The name also makes sense. Future ["Nova Lake-HX"](/noticias/componentes/intel-nova-lake-filtracion-4970k/) would arrive under the Core Ultra 4000HX denomination, so Intel would remove "Ultra" and lower the series to Core 2000HX to separate chips from 18A (the new ones) from the Intel 7 dies that "Raptor Lake Next" would inherit. This also avoids confusion with Arrow Lake-HX, which already uses the Core Ultra 200HX brand.

Intel's HX family has a known history: it arrived in 2023 with the 13th-generation Core i9-13980HX and was refreshed in 2024 with the 14th-generation Core i9-14900HX. That same year, top-end SKUs of "Raptor Lake Refresh" also appeared, such as the Core 7 150U and the Core 9 270H, and for this year something is expected in the form of a Core 9 2xxxHX.

## Why Intel returns to Raptor Lake

The main reason is memory price. The Core Ultra "Arrow Lake" generation eliminated DDR4 compatibility, so anyone who doesn't want —or can't afford— to pay for DDR5 amidst the price crisis ends up without a modern Intel platform. "Raptor Lake Next" would serve precisely that: keeping DDR4 alive in high-end equipment, both on LGA-1700 desktops and now, laptops.

In exchange, the bet gives up more advanced efficiency. Returning to an Intel 7 node in an HX laptop has direct consequences for something much more tangible than a benchmark table: thermals and battery life. The node is already very mature —which helps cost and performance per clock— but it doesn't compete in watts with the most recent lithographies.

## What changes for the reader

For those looking for a powerful laptop without paying the DDR5 premium, this leak points to an interesting scenario: gaming or creation equipment with cheaper memory and multi-core performance that, on paper, would not drop below current 24 threads. It is essentially the same play Intel is preparing for desktops, [as already leaked for the successor of the i5-14600K](/noticias/intel-raptor-lake-next-8p/).

However, anyone prioritizing battery life and efficiency —the laptop profile that lasts through the day without a charger— should look elsewhere: an HX chip on Intel 7 is not the natural way to squeeze battery. And it's worth remembering that all of this is a leak without confirmation from Intel: there are no prices, dates, or official SKUs yet.
