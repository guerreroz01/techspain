Saying "can you hear me?" or "hello?" over and over during a call is one of the
clearest signs that the call audio on your Android phone needs attention. With a
few quick tweaks and settings that are not all that hidden, that audio can
improve noticeably.

There are several ways to get there, and no, it does not boil down to cleaning
the microphone (though you should do that too). Features such as Clear Calling,
Wi-Fi calling, VoLTE, Adapt sound and Mic mode all help calls sound much
crisper. As with everything Android, there is no single way of doing things:
features and menus vary by device.

This guide covers call audio. If what you want is better sound for music or
videos, we have another guide on
[how to get the best audio quality on Spotify](/noticias/spotify-best-audio-quality/).

One clarification comes first: call quality does not depend only on your phone.
If the person on the other end has a weak signal, a poor microphone or a noisy
room, the problem will affect you all the same. What follows focuses on the
elements you can actually control.

## Prerequisites

- An Android phone in working order. Nothing needs to be installed.
- Note that menu paths are shown in English, exactly as they appear in the
  Engadget original: the exact label changes with the device language and each
  manufacturer's skin.
- Check that your specific model supports the feature. Several of these options
  are exclusive to particular tiers (each step says which).

## Steps to improve call sound quality

### 1. Turn on VoLTE (and VoNR) for calls over a high-speed network

VoLTE, Voice over LTE, is as simple as its name suggests: the call is made over
a high-speed 4G data network. (The equivalent connectivity over 5G is called
Voice over New Radio, or VoNR.) Even in 2026, some calls still happen over older
2G and 3G networks, since those were built to carry voice traffic, early 4G
could not carry phone calls, and the VoLTE rollout is still incomplete. The
higher speeds of VoLTE and VoNR deliver clearer sound, HD voice and faster
connections: calls connect much more quickly than on older networks.

To turn it on, on a generic Android device: `Settings > Network & Internet > Mobile network > VoLTE calls` (some phones label this as HD Calling or 4G Calling). On Samsung devices: `Settings > Connections > Mobile Networks > VoLTE calls` or `VoNR`.

### 2. Turn on Wi-Fi calling

Turning on Wi-Fi calling is another simple way to improve your Android phone's
call quality. The feature routes calls through your home or office Wi-Fi:
instead of sending your voice over the cellular network, it converts it into
small digital packets. Those are sent securely over Wi-Fi to your carrier's
network, where the call is processed and handed over to the other person.
Thanks to extra coverage, almost no cellular obstructions and far less network
congestion, Wi-Fi calling delivers better quality than cellular networks can.

To turn it on: `Settings > Network & internet > SIMS > Carrier SIM > Wi-Fi Calling`. On Samsung the path is different: `Settings > Connections > Wi-Fi Calling > Toggle on Wi-Fi Calling`.

### 3. Cut background noise with Clear Calling or Mic mode

Many Android phone makers have built solutions for reducing background noise on
calls, even though each of them names them differently.

On Pixel phones the feature is called **Clear Calling**: it improves your voice
quality and filters background noise so the call audio sounds better. To turn it
on, open `Settings > Sound & vibration > Clear Calling > Toggle on Use Clear Calling`.

You can also switch it on mid-call: press any volume button and select Clear
Calling in the volume panel. For now the feature is only available on Pixel 7
and later. In VoIP apps such as WhatsApp and Zoom, Clear Calling only works on
Pixel 10 and later, excluding the Pixel 10a.

Newer Samsung devices have a similar feature called **Mic mode**. To use it you
need to already be on a call: once it starts, open the Quick settings panel and
tap Mic mode at the top. The feature offers three options:

- **Standard**: applies minimal audio filtering.
- **Voice focus**: increases audio filtering so you can focus on the voice of
  the person calling you.
- **Full spectrum**: captures both the voice and background sounds.

Tap **Done** to apply your choice and continue the call. Mic mode is only
available on select newer Galaxy devices and works on both regular calls and
VoIP apps, though the menu options can change depending on the calling app.

### 4. Tune the audio to your ears with Adapt sound or Adaptive Sound

Sometimes a small adjustment is all it takes to enjoy satisfying call audio.
**Adapt sound** on Samsung phones tunes the device's audio output to your
particular ears: it does not filter noise or boost the volume as such. While
active, Adapt sound adjusts the device's audio to compensate for the parts of
the sound spectrum you struggle to hear clearly.

To find it: `Settings > Sounds and vibration > Sound quality and effects > Adapt sound`. On that page you can either pick one of the age-based presets or take a short hearing test to fully customize your phone's speakers to your own ears. Bear in mind that it only works while you are wearing headphones.

Pixel devices have a similar feature, **Adaptive Sound**, which uses the phone's
microphone to automatically adjust the speaker's sound depending on the
environment. For example, if you take a call in a noisy place, it may boost the
mid and high frequencies on its own so you can hear the conversation better.
Other Android phones may have their own version of Adapt sound (or Adaptive
sound), so it is worth rummaging through the Settings app (or doing a quick
search) if your phone is neither a Pixel nor a Samsung.

### 5. Check the hardware: dust, lint and the case

If none of the fixes above has worked, it is worth checking whether dust, lint
or even the case is obstructing the phone's microphone or speaker. Any of these
affects call quality, whatever setting you tweak.

## How to verify that call audio improved

The check is straightforward: place a test call to someone you trust and ask
whether you sound better than before, especially if you turned on Clear Calling
or Mic mode, which exist precisely so that you are heard more clearly. If the
person still reports the same problem, try switching the filtering mode (from
Voice focus to Full spectrum, for example) and repeat the test.

You can also verify that Wi-Fi calling or VoLTE is working by making a call in
an area with weak cellular coverage: if the conversation stays clear where it
used to drop, the feature is doing its job.

## Limitations and caveats

- **There is no single solution for everyone.** As elsewhere on Android,
  features and menus vary by device: what sits in one place on one model sits
  somewhere else on another.
- **The other person counts too.** If the caller has a weak signal, a poor
  microphone or a noisy environment, that will affect the call no matter what
  you do with your own phone.
- **Limited availability.** Clear Calling reaches Pixel 7 and later; in VoIP
  apps such as WhatsApp or Zoom only Pixel 10 and later, without the Pixel 10a.
  Mic mode is limited to select newer Galaxy devices. Adapt sound only works
  with headphones connected.
- **Cleaning the microphone is still recommended.** These settings do not
  replace basic cleaning of the phone's hardware.
