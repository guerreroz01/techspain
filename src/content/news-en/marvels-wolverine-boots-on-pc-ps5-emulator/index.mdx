**Marvel’s Wolverine**, the new game from Insomniac Games, landed on PlayStation 5 only days ago —we covered it in the article on [the launch of Marvel’s Wolverine on PS5](/noticias/marvels-wolverine-ps5-launch/)— and it is already executable on PC thanks to emulation. A group of modders has managed to get the title to boot on the platform through **KytyPS5**, the PS5 emulator, although for now it only reaches the title screen, with performance that prevents normal play. It is the first PS5 release to hit the market after Sony decided not to bring its exclusives to PC, which makes projects like this one especially interesting for those without a console.

## Marvel’s Wolverine boots on PC, but it is not yet playable

We mean “boot” in the strict sense: the game starts, but it is far from playable. On a system built around a **Ryzen 7 7800X3D** and a **GeForce RTX 5090**, the title ran at **just 2 FPS**, a figure insufficient for any kind of real playthrough.

The cover image, published by the original outlet, shows exactly that emulator debug overlay, with the game’s identification, version `01.001.005` and the hardware used —the same Ryzen 7 7800X3D and RTX 5090— along with the “Press ✕ to start” label of the opening menu.

Even so, the milestone is no small one: that a freshly released game is already executable through an emulator says a great deal about the pace of this project, currently led by **KytyPS5**. PlayStation 5 emulation has gone from being a distant promise to showing the console’s own games running on PC in a matter of days.

## The same emulator scales with Demon’s Souls

The progress is not limited to Wolverine. The **Demon’s Souls** remake went from running at **1 fps to 30 fps** in just a few days, a jump [we followed at the time](/noticias/demons-souls-remake-ps5-emulator-30fps/), and the latest update puts that mark between **40 and 50 FPS** on an **Intel Core i9-14900K** and an **RTX 5090**.

VideoCardz, the source of this information, also published on September 29 an update on Wolverine: the game already goes beyond the menus in KytyPS5 and renders cinematics and actual gameplay, with new screenshots showing it in action. In other words, within a few hours the situation went from “it reaches the title screen” to “you can see it being played.”

It is worth qualifying that PS5 emulation is still a long way from offering playable performance for the general public. What matters is the speed of progress over recent months, not today’s frame rate.

## The push from modders and artificial intelligence

Two factors sit behind these leaps. The first is the continuous effort of the modders, who are putting in hours to debug every error that prevents titles from running.

The second is **artificial intelligence**, which the outlet identifies as a key lever for emulation. AI agents allow the software to be debugged continuously in just a few seconds, making it easier to find the path that lets a game boot. They also analyse errors quickly, diagnose what failed and help fix it. In practice, they become a supporting tool for emulator development.

## What changes for the reader

For anyone who wants to try Marvel’s Wolverine outside the PS5, the answer is still “not yet”: 2 FPS is not playable and no current configuration makes up for that figure. What does change is the trajectory. In very little time, a brand-new exclusive already boots on PC, Demon’s Souls approaches 50 FPS and the game begins to show real content. If the trend holds, the conversation about PS5 emulation will stop being about “whether” it will work and start being about “when.”
