Cambridge Audio is not replacing its most affordable true wireless earbuds, it is refining them. The **Melomania A100 SE** is a special edition of the A100 that arrived in 2025, and it keeps its **$149** price tag with three concrete changes: it adds **Dolby Audio** with spatial audio, introduces revised tuning and brings a blue finish to the existing colour range.

It is a sensible lineup decision: keep what already worked and touch only what the listener actually perceives. Anyone who already owns the A100 has no reason to switch; anyone shopping for earbuds under $150 has one more reason to take a look.

## Dolby Audio, new grilles and tuning

The most visible addition is **Dolby Audio with Spatial Audio**, enabled from the **Melomania Connect** app (iOS and Android). Cambridge Audio says it improves vocal and dialogue clarity while widening the sense of space. One detail the company has confirmed is worth clearing up: there is **no Dolby Atmos support** in the feature set, however much the name invites confusion.

The second change is more analogue: each earbud's **grille is now less restrictive**, which the brand says adds treble "airiness". On top of that comes **revised acoustic tuning** promising deeper bass, a richer midrange and clearer highs than the original A100. Cosmetically, the SE adds a **blue finish** alongside black and white.

## What carries over on the Melomania A100 SE

The hardware foundation is unchanged, and that matters because it is where the original model's value lay. It keeps **Class A/B amplification**, unusual in fully wireless earbuds at this price, paired with **10 mm drivers using neodymium magnets** and a **dual-core 240 MHz Qualcomm Kalimba DSP** handling extra signal processing to reduce distortion. The processor is the **Qualcomm QCC3091** with **Snapdragon Sound** support.

On the wireless side it retains **Bluetooth 5.4**, multipoint, Google Fast Pair and latency under **80 ms** in Gaming Mode from the app. Supported codecs are **aptX Lossless, aptX Adaptive, LDAC, AAC and SBC**, with **24-bit/96 kHz** resolution.

Battery life carries over too: up to **39 hours** in total with the case, dropping to around **21 hours** with noise cancelling on. Charging is via **USB-C or wirelessly** (Qi-compatible but not certified), and a **10-minute** fast charge delivers about **3.2 hours** of playback with cancelling off. The set packs **six microphones** (three per earbud), **Adaptive Hybrid ANC**, a transparency mode, wind noise reduction and capacitive wear detection. Everything is adjusted from the app through a **7-band EQ**.

## Price and availability

The **Cambridge Audio Melomania A100 SE** is available now from Cambridge Audio's website and authorized retailers for **$149**, the same launch price as the A100. The original model, already discounted below $100, remains on sale.

![The Melomania A100 SE charging cases in their black, white and blue finishes](./assets/case-colors.jpg)

Is it worth it? For anyone who does not yet own earbuds in this segment, the combination of **Class A/B amplification with LDAC and aptX Lossless at $149** remains hard to match. The SE's appeal is not a generational leap but refinements you notice in daily use: a little more treble air and supposedly better-balanced sound. As a reference point for what this segment offers, the JLab Epic Sport ANC 3 also lean on LDAC and ANC, though with a sporty focus and a lower price.
