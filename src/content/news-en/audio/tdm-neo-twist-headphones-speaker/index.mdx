The TDM Neo are on sale now for $249. They are a pair of over-ear headphones that, by twisting the earcups on the headband, switch to working as a portable Bluetooth speaker rated at 5 W per channel. The idea covers two situations that normally call for two devices with a single product: personal listening on the commute and shared music in a small group. TDM bills them as the first high-fidelity headphones able to transform into a speaker with a single twist.

## How the TDM Neo switches to speaker mode

The mode change does not rely on removable parts or a separate accessory: the mechanism is built into the headband itself, and rotating the earcups is enough to move the device from headphones to speaker. The audio system relies on four 40 mm drivers: two face inward for personal listening and two face outward for playback in speaker mode.

![The TDM Neo twisting from headphone mode to speaker mode](./assets/tdm-neo-giro.jpg)

That separation of drivers is the interesting part of the design. Rather than reusing the same transducers pointed in another direction, TDM devotes two units to each use, which on paper avoids the compromise of a single set that must sound good in two different physical positions. In speaker mode the two outer drivers deliver 5 W per side, enough for a small room, though the actual result will depend on the size of the space and where the device is placed.

## What is measured and what is not

Connectivity is Bluetooth 6.0 with multipoint, so the Neo can hold a link to several devices and jump between sources without re-pairing. On codecs, TDM stops at SBC and AAC: there is no trace of aptX or LDAC, nor is active noise cancellation (ANC) announced. What it does include is passive isolation and a multi-array microphone system with environmental noise cancellation (ENC) that works in both modes, for private calls or to use the device as a conference speaker.

![The TDM Neo in speaker mode, with the outer drivers running](./assets/tdm-neo-modo-altavoz.jpg)

Battery life is striking: more than 200 hours in headphone mode and up to 16 hours in speaker mode. Charging is over USB-C, with about three hours for a full charge and up to eight hours of playback after five minutes plugged in. The whole unit weighs 340 grams, a contained figure considering it carries drivers, amplification and a battery for two modes. The headband is adjustable, the cushions are dual-layer memory foam and the battery module is replaceable. There are four physical buttons for power, volume and mode switching, and the twist action can be customized from the free app (iOS and Android), which adds separate EQs for each mode, Game Mode, device location and power management.

## Is it worth it?

TDM is not the first brand to try to merge headphones and a speaker; similar offerings from HyperGear and Sharper Image already existed. The Neo's difference lies in the patented mechanism, the four dedicated drivers and the separate amplification, which make it a more ambitious take on the concept. The cost of that versatility shows up in the specs: anyone looking for noise cancellation or high-resolution codecs — common in alternatives such as the [Technics EAH-A1000](/noticias/technics-eah-a1000/) — will not find them here.

![The TDM Neo in white](./assets/tdm-neo-blanco.jpg)

At $249, in black and white finishes and with a carry case included, the Neo makes sense for someone who genuinely wants a single device for personal and shared listening and does not mind giving up what it lacks. For everyone else, buying a good pair of headphones and a speaker separately still makes more sense.
