The Sennheiser Momentum 4 Wireless are priced at $205 on Amazon, as reported by XDA Developers. The model's list price is $300, so the discount is roughly one third off the sticker: a steep cut for a pair of headphones that tend to hold their value.

It is worth separating the street price from the catalogue price. Sennheiser's own store keeps the Momentum 4 at $229.95, also reduced from the $299.95 list price. The Amazon deal lands below even the price the manufacturer applies on its own website, something that does not happen with every seasonal discount. The product listing that accompanies the story also links a unit at $214 at Best Buy, so it pays to compare before completing the purchase.

## Momentum 4: battery life, ANC and comfort at this price

The Momentum 4's main argument remains the battery. Sennheiser claims up to 60 hours of playback with active noise cancellation enabled, measured with an iPhone at medium volume. That figure puts the model ahead of a good part of its direct rivals and changes the routine: instead of charging it every two or three days, charging becomes a weekly chore. Fast charging adds about six hours of listening in ten minutes.

The cancellation is hybrid and adaptive, with four microphones, and it is paired with a Transparency mode that lets outside sound through without taking the headphones off. In daily use, that is the difference between a useful travel headphone and one that only performs in a quiet room: the system adjusts isolation to the surrounding noise instead of applying a fixed level.

On comfort, XDA notes that the design is more minimalist than the previous generation and that it prioritises long sessions, with deep ear cushions and a padded headband. The manufacturer adds a low-friction hinge that spreads pressure across the head. The package includes a carry case, a USB-C charging cable and an audio cable with 3.5 mm and 2.5 mm connectors for wired listening.

## Codecs and connectivity: what to check before buying

The Momentum 4 uses Bluetooth 5.2 and supports SBC, AAC, aptX and aptX Adaptive, according to Sennheiser's official specifications. The practical consequence is that maximum quality also depends on the phone: if the handset does not implement aptX Adaptive, the headphone will fall back to a more basic codec. Anyone coming from a model with a USB dongle may see a step back on that specific point; the company's HDB 630 solves that problem with an external adapter, and we covered it in [this piece on the HDB 630](/noticias/audio/sennheiser-hdb-630/).

The Smart Control app adds an equaliser, presets and sound customisation, plus auto power-on, automatic pause when the headphones are removed and playback resume. These are convenience features that are welcome every day and that are not always present on models in the same price bracket.

## Who should buy it and who should not

It is worth it if you are looking for an over-ear headphone to listen to music for many hours, with adaptive isolation and battery life that lets you forget about the charger. Also if you value Sennheiser's sound and want to enter its high-end range without paying full price: at $205, the outlay comes close to that of mid-range models.

It is not the best option if spatial audio is a priority. The Momentum 4 does not include that feature, while the Momentum 5 from the same company does offer it with Dolby Atmos. Nor is it ideal if the main use is calls: it has four beamforming microphones with wind suppression, but it is not an office headphone with a boom microphone, and it competes at a disadvantage there.

## The limitation you have to accept

XDA warns of two physical details: the ear cushions can build up heat during long sessions, and the clamping pressure is somewhat firmer than that of some competitors. On top of that, the model has no IP rating, so it is not designed for sweating at the gym or for working in the rain.

## Is it worth it?

At $205, the Momentum 4 holds up on battery life, isolation and comfort, and loses ground only on features that are not part of its proposition. If spatial audio or better call performance are requirements, there are alternatives; if what you want is to listen for hours with solid cancellation, the deal is hard to match. XDA warns that this kind of discount tends to sell out quickly.
