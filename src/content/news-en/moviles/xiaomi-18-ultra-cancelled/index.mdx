The Xiaomi 18 Ultra would never reach stores. According to leaker Digital Chat Station, the company had the model in development but reportedly scrapped it for a reason that is unusual for a flagship: the resulting price would be too high and sales would not hold up. Xiaomi has neither confirmed nor denied the cancellation, so this points to a weighty rumour, not an official announcement.

The detail is ironic, because what would have sunk the phone is exactly what was meant to sell it: its camera system. The better the hardware, the more expensive it is to make, and on a flagship that extra cost is passed straight onto the price tag. If the leak is accurate, Xiaomi decided the Spanish and European market was not ready to pay that bill.

## An Ultra dropped over price, not over ambition

The report ties into rumours that were already circulating in August and points to an internal review: the phone was on the table, but the maths did not work. Production costs — made worse by record-high memory and component prices — would have pushed the final bill to a point where Xiaomi reportedly preferred not to compete.

It is not the first time a manufacturer trims its line-up to protect margins. The difference here is that the most desirable variant of the family, the one that usually sets the brand's technical ceiling, is the one being dropped, leaving the flagship role to the models it does plan to ship.

## What the Xiaomi 18 Ultra would have packed

The leaks sketch out a phone loaded to the brim. The headline act was a triple 200-megapixel camera across all three lenses:

- A 1/1.12-inch LOFIC main sensor.
- A 1/1.56-inch ultra-wide.
- A 1/1.28-inch LOFIC periscope telephoto with continuous optical zoom, compatible with a teleconverter.

On top of that would come a flat display with bezels of barely 1 mm, a top-end Qualcomm chip, symmetrical dual stereo speakers and a high-capacity vibration motor. LOFIC deserves a pause: it is a technology that gives each pixel an extra charge reservoir, so scenes with a big gap between light and shadow — a sunset, a neon sign at night — neither blow out the bright areas nor lose detail in the dark ones. It is exactly the kind of sensor that justifies a premium, and also the one that inflates the bill.

## What changes for anyone after a flagship

The Xiaomi 18 series would have been trimmed to three models: Xiaomi 18, 18 Pro and 18 Pro Max. The unveiling of the Pro line already pointed that way, and the 18 Pro Max would now be the one carrying the brand's highest tier with global availability.

![Official image of the Xiaomi 18 Pro series, the line that would take over as the brand's flagship](./assets/xiaomi-18-pro.jpg)

For buyers, the takeaway is simple: if you wanted the Ultra for its camera, there will be no model to replace it at the same level this year. The good news is that the Ultra family is not disappearing for good; the same reports say the brand would keep working on a Xiaomi 19 Ultra, though the leap would slip to 2027 at the earliest. At this price, and with the camera as its only real differentiator, waiting for the next generation still looks like the sensible call.
