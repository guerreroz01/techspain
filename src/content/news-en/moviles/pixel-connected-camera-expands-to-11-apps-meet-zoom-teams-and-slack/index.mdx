Anyone recording video or streaming with a Pixel now has one less excuse to turn on their laptop. Google has begun rolling out an update to Pixel Camera Services that brings the Connected Camera feature to 11 communication and productivity apps, including Google Meet, Zoom, Microsoft Teams and Slack. With this move, the number of officially compatible apps jumps from five to sixteen.

## What Has Changed with Pixel Connected Camera

Connected Camera allows a Pixel to use another device's camera as an additional source: it can serve a second linked Pixel or even a GoPro. The difference from other solutions is that the feed retains the native effects and filters of the app from which it originates, so you don't have to give them up when switching cameras.

In practice, it works like a small television studio: you switch from camera 1 to camera 2 without cutting the broadcast. Until now that capability was limited to a handful of apps; this batch adds eleven at once. Google confirms names like Meet, Zoom, Teams and Slack, raising the total number of compatible apps to sixteen.

## Why It Matters

Multi-camera video is no longer the domain of expensive gear, but software remained the bottleneck: having a second source is pointless if the app you use daily doesn't accept it. Expanding the compatibility list is what turns a curious feature into something usable in a real work call or live stream, without resorting to a PC and mixing software.

For the Spanish-speaking audience, the appeal is mostly practical: it's a feature that was already included in the phone and is now activated in more places where it is actually used, rather than remaining a lab demo.

## What Changes for the Reader

The update arrives through the Pixel Camera Services app on the Play Store, so you don't need to buy anything new: it installs like any other Google package. That said, there is a hardware requirement that doesn't disappear: the feature needs a second compatible device —another Pixel or a GoPro— to provide the additional shot. Without that second device, connected camera has no second camera.

## Verdict

At this price, yes: the update is free and expands something the phone already knew how to do. The real cost isn't in the app, but in the second device, so the feature only makes sense if you already have two Pixels or a GoPro on hand. Buying an extra mobile solely for connected camera doesn't make sense; making use of it if it's already part of your setup does.
