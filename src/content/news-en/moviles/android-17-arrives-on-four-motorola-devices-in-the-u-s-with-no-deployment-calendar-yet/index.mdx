# Android 17 arrives on four Motorola devices in the U.S., with no deployment calendar yet

The update policy is the most important factor when someone considers buying a Motorola, so every advance in its rollout deserves attention. The company has brought Android 17 to four devices in the United States in just a couple of days, with the Razr Ultra (2026) as the most prominent name in this batch. The data that matters for buyers comes at the end: no calendar has been published yet, so there is no way to predict when each model will get its turn.

## Which phones receive Android 17

The Razr Ultra (2026) leads this first wave. The update arrives via OTA with a 4.17 GB package, so it’s advisable to download it over a fast Wi-Fi network before confirming. Once installed, the device moves to firmware version A171WLH.38–19–5, and the security patch level remains at September 2026.

The original Motorola Signature also appears among the first receivers in the United States, and it is additionally receiving the update in India. The other surprise lies in the mid-range: the Edge 60 Pro and Edge 60 Fusion have been updated alongside the more expensive models, and they even started receiving the package a few days earlier.

![Motorola phone with the Android 17 beta version](./assets/motorola-android-17-beta.png)

## Why it matters: mid-range and support policy

That two mid-range phones are updated at the same time—or before—the company’s most expensive foldable is the relevant detail in this batch. The rollout isn’t limited to premium models, something that doesn’t always happen among manufacturers, and the Edge 60 Pro and Fusion demonstrate that Motorola has included part of its affordable catalog at launch.

Another point worth watching is support. The changelog accompanying these devices is very brief, and the company maintains an official page detailing what Android 17 includes. For those following the cycle from Spain, the contrast is clear: [Samsung already deployed Android 17 in Europe with One UI 9](/noticias/one-ui-9-disponible/), while Motorola’s advance of which there is record is for the United States and, in the case of the Signature, also India. In parallel, the testing branch continues its course with the [Android 17 QPR3 betas](/noticias/moviles/android-17-qpr3-beta-1/) on compatible Pixels.

## What changes for the reader

If you have one of these models, the update will arrive in batches, so it may take some time to appear. To check manually, go to Settings and tap System Update. If your device isn’t on the list, Motorola itself maintains an official list of eligible devices for Android 17 that’s worth reviewing before assuming you’ll be left out.

The fine print remains the calendar: since no dates have been published, it’s impossible to promise when deployment will reach each market or confirm timelines for Spain. Anyone buying a Motorola thinking in terms of years of support should consult that list of eligible devices before deciding; rollout is advancing, yes, but without a calendar there’s no guarantee of when each phone will get its turn.
