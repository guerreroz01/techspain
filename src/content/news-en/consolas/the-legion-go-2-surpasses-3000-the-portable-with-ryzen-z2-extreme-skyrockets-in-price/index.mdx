Lenovo has touched the price of its flagship portable console again and this time it has crossed an uncomfortable barrier. The Legion Go 2 with Ryzen Z2 Extreme, 32 GB of memory and 2 TB of storage is already available in the company's US store for $3,081.99, compared to the last adjustment of $2,849. That is about $233 more, close to an 8% increase, and leaves the machine above $3,000.

![Lenovo Logo](./assets/lenovo-logo.png)

## A Rise That Accumulates in the Legion Go 2

It is not the first move. The Legion Go 2 with Z2 Extreme started at $1,349.99 and had already moved to $1,999.99. The 32 GB and 2 TB configuration, the most expensive one, scaled up later to $2,849 and now settles at $3,081.99. The small family is not spared either: the Legion Go S went from $899.99 to $1,579.99 in its Z1 Extreme model and from $649.99 to $989.99 in the Z2 Go variant.

The 1 TB version of the Legion Go 2 can still be found at Best Buy for $1,999.99, a difference of more than a thousand dollars between two stores for the same product. Lenovo also keeps the console out of stock in its own store while affiliated retailers do have units, and has not officially announced the reason for the adjustment.

## Memory Behind the New Price

The most plausible explanation is not in the console but in the components. The sector has been dragging months of increases due to the rising cost of memory and DRAM, the phenomenon already known as the "RAMpocalypse" that has hit much PC hardware. The Legion Go 2 mounts 32 GB of soldered LPDDR5X, so there is no margin to cut in that section. It is the same Z2 Extreme chip family that debuts with the [ROG Xbox Ally X 20th Anniversary Edition](/noticias/rog-xbox-ally-x-20-aniversario-oled/), another recent launch that also lands with high prices.

To this is added that AMD prepares a price increase of around 10% on graphics cards, chipsets and possibly processors, scheduled for the fourth quarter of 2026. The calendar fits with this mid-October adjustment, suggesting that Best Buy's $1,999.99 will last only as long as units remain at that price.

## What Remains for the Player

For those who see the console as a purchase, the move changes little in technical appeal but much in the bank account. The Legion Go 2 remains a portable with an 8.8-inch OLED screen at 144 Hz, 32 GB of memory and Ryzen Z2 Extreme, but above $3,000 it now competes with full gaming laptops, and even with a desktop and a living room console together.

The practical reading is simple: if the interest lies in this generation, the 1 TB unit at $1,999.99 at an affiliated store is today the least penalized option, always with the warning that that stock can run out quickly. And for the coming months, it is advisable to assume that portable prices will follow memory's trail: whoever waits for a short-term reduction may end up waiting.
