The **EasySMX S10 Lite** is seeking a place among budget Nintendo Switch 2 controllers: the official store lists it at **36.95 euros** with Spain and EUR selected, as checked on October 30. For that amount, it combines Hall-sensor sticks, motion controls and the ability to wake the console from the controller. However, [Jessica Conditt's Engadget review, published on October 29](https://www.engadget.com/2294413/easysmx-s10-lite-review/), identifies two significant trade-offs: a faceplate that does not fit properly on her unit and stick clicks that are too stiff for her liking.

This is not TechSpain24's own test. The usage impressions come from that review, which awards it 7 out of 10; the European price and update notice have been checked against the manufacturer.

## What the EasySMX S10 Lite offers for gaming

The proposition goes beyond replacing Joy-Con controllers when playing on a television. The S10 Lite includes two configurable rear buttons, a C button for GameChat and four-level adjustable vibration. EasySMX confirms Bluetooth or wired connectivity and compatibility with Switch 2, the original Switch, Windows, Android and iOS. The specifications also list a 1,000 mAh battery and USB-C charging, but that capacity alone does not tell you how many hours a gaming session will last.

Conditt highlights the smooth feel of the sticks as they reach the edge of their travel and the pronounced response of the microswitch buttons. She also appreciates its ability to wake the Switch 2 and its nine-axis motion controls. These are practical features for anyone seeking a second controller without giving up motion control or having to approach the console to wake it.

The nuance lies in preferences and the type of button press. The journalist finds the stick clicks stiff and notes that the triggers use microswitches, not analog travel with Hall sensors. Do not confuse the sensors in the sticks with those in the triggers: sharing a controller does not mean sharing technology.

## The magnetic cover is the most disputed point

The translucent faceplate is attached with magnets and can be removed. On the unit reviewed by Engadget, the bottom arrived improperly seated and, after repositioning it, one side still protruded. That observation describes the review sample; it does not prove that every unit has the same fit.

The other objection concerns customization. The official website promotes magnetic covers and stickers as part of a user-modification proposition. Conditt criticizes the brand for not selling alternative faceplates or stickers to deliver on that idea when she conducted her review. A removable cover should therefore not be interpreted as guaranteeing a catalog of decorative replacements available to buy.

The cover photograph comes from Engadget. The light blue finish leaves the faceplate magnets visible; a pink version also exists, according to the product listing and review.

## Before buying: final price and updating

The **36.95 euros** is the amount shown on the [official S10 Lite listing](https://www.easysmx.com/products/easysmx-s10lite-switch2-controller), not a conversion of the usual 40-dollar price cited by Engadget. The store says shipping, taxes and discounts are calculated at checkout. Check the total and the terms for your chosen destination before treating that amount as the final delivered cost.

More importantly for support, the listing itself links to a [firmware notice to fix Switch 2 compatibility](https://www.easysmx.com/pages/easysmx-s10-lite-firmware-update-switch-2-compatibility-fix). EasySMX presents updating via PC as available and the app alternative as pending. The manufacturer says it fixes connection and disconnection problems following updates; do not assume that every unit arrives already updated.

## A second controller with specific compromises

The S10 Lite's appeal lies in combining a modest price, Hall sensors and remote wake-up, not in promising the same experience as any more expensive controller. If you are comparing options, our article on the [Turtle Beach Rematch for Switch 2](/noticias/turtle-beach-rematch-switch-2-review/) provides another approach to controls and design, without making the two products equivalent.

For an informed purchase, physical fit and button feel matter as much as the advertised features. The review makes its reservations clear, and the official notice adds a necessary support check: confirm how to update the controller and review return terms before choosing it to accompany the console.
