The Steam Deck remains the benchmark among PC gaming handhelds, and every leak about its successor gets careful reading. The latest one points to an AMD APU named «Gainsborough» that would combine Zen 6 cores, RDNA 5 graphics and a 3-nanometer node. It is worth saying this from the start: this is a rumor, and neither AMD nor Valve has confirmed it, so none of the details below should be taken as an announcement.

## A semi-custom APU called «Gainsborough»

The YouTube channel Moore's Law is Dead is back with a video about Valve's future console, reported by El Chapuzas Informático. The leak, attributed to a user on X, points to the appearance of an APU called «Gainsborough» in AMD's drivers and internal documentation. It is described as a semi-custom APU designed by AMD's S3 team for clients, and it would not be intended for laptops from other brands.

According to that same information, it would be built on TSMC's N3P 3-nanometer node, and the team that would have developed it also worked on the PlayStation consoles. The main clue about its destination is the name: the original Steam Deck's APU was called «Aerith», and «Gainsborough» is the surname of the same Final Fantasy VII protagonist, so together they form the character's full name. It is a suggestive nod, but nothing more.

## What performance is claimed for this Steam Deck 2

If the leak holds up, the most ambitious configuration would point to between 4 and 8 Zen 6C cores, between 12 and 24 RDNA 5 compute units and N3P manufacturing, with performance similar to the future handheld PS6. The leap over the original Steam Deck would be notable: where the current model sits in the orbit of a PS4, this one would land closer to a PS5, especially in scenes with ray tracing and upscaling, where the RDNA 5 generation gains the upper hand.

![Illustration about the Steam Deck 2 and the RAM shortage](./assets/steam-deck-2-escasez-ram.jpg)

The leak adds details about the rumored handheld PS6, which would use a «Canis» APU also built on N3P, with 4 Zen 6C cores and 2 Zen 6 LP cores, support for up to 48 GB of 192-bit LPDDR5X RAM and a 16-CU iGPU running at 1.2 GHz in handheld mode and 1.65 GHz in dock mode. For the Steam Deck 2, the launch window being discussed is late 2027 to early 2028.

## Why the catalog matters more than the teraflop war

Read cold, the rumor promises the kind of leap Valve has been clamoring for for years. The company has repeatedly said it will not take the step to a second generation until the hardware represents a real evolution rather than a simple power increase at the expense of battery life. That is why the key question is not how many teraflops «Gainsborough» would add, but what it lets you run: an RDNA 5 jump in ray tracing and upscaling translates into games that demand compromises today, not a number to brag about.

It is worth remembering that Valve's line remains cautious and that hardware progress does not always arrive when expected. The company itself acknowledged a few weeks ago that it is still working out «how and when» to unveil the successor, as we reported when we covered its remarks on [when the Steam Deck 2 will arrive](/noticias/steam-deck-2-how-when/). The catalog, verified compatibility and support for the current generation weigh more today than an unconfirmed spec sheet.

## What it means for buyers

There is no official confirmation from AMD, Valve or Sony, and the 2024 rumors that placed the console in late 2026 have not come true, so the successor still looks a long way off. For anyone who wants to play now, the current Steam Deck keeps working and retains the manufacturer's support; for anyone willing to wait, the leak sketches a handheld closer to a PS5, but with no date, no price and the usual risk that comes with leaks. Buying on the strength of a rumor is never a good plan: the decision should come when real hardware exists and a catalog can take advantage of it.
