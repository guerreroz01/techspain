The PlayStation 5 has received Quick Spectral Super Resolution (QSSR), a new AI upscaling technology Sony is using to improve image detail and stability without depending on the dedicated hardware found in the PS5 Pro. The company presents it as a new performance tier within its reconstruction solutions, and its first users are already two very specific games: *Marvel's Wolverine* and *Ghost of Yōtei*, which receive it as an additional graphics option.

## What QSSR is and how it differs from PSSR

QSSR is born from Project Amethyst, Sony and AMD's AI graphics collaboration, and relies on two innovations: a more streamlined neural network architecture and a hand-tuned implementation to get the most out of the PS5's hardware. That second point is key, because the standard console lacks the dedicated machine-learning accelerators found in the PS5 Pro.

That is why QSSR is not simply PSSR running on a 2020 PS5. Both solutions share an origin and a goal, but they are designed for different hardware capabilities: PSSR remains [the quality benchmark on the PS5 Pro](/noticias/sony-pssr-2-firmware/), while QSSR gives up part of that headroom to run efficiently on the original machine. The goal is the same as ever in an aging generation: hold 60 frames per second while cleaning up an image that starts from lower internal resolutions.

## The first games to use it

[*Marvel's Wolverine*](/noticias/marvels-wolverine-ps5-launch/) and *Ghost of Yōtei* are the two titles debuting QSSR. Insomniac Games highlights that it adds pixel-level clarity and stability in environments packed with small details, such as the streets of Madripoor or the alleys of Shinjuku. Sucker Punch stresses its ability to resolve fine elements with a temporal stability that, according to the studio, had not been possible on the standard console until now.

In both cases the goal is not to transform the graphics, but to refine an image that starts from a lower resolution without losing the performance that makes it possible. Sony has also confirmed that QSSR will be available to all PlayStation studios, which opens the door to more games adopting it going forward.

## What changes for the player

The most visible difference is in the final resolution. On the PS5 Pro, developers usually reconstruct the image up to 4K while lowering the internal resolution, whereas on the standard console the cost of raising the output is much higher because there are no specific accelerators. MuyComputer's analysis suggests that 1440p may become a much more common reconstruction target with QSSR, with studios always deciding which combination of render and output resolution best suits each game.

QSSR does not turn a PS5 into a PS5 Pro: the Pro keeps its edge with a more powerful GPU, dedicated machine-learning hardware and a PSSR capable of targeting more ambitious resolution goals. The interesting part is something else. Sony adding, through software, a capability that was not planned when it designed the console in 2020 helps the machine age better just as the generation enters its final stretch and games become more demanding. It will not make a PS5 behave like a Pro, but it will help it hold up for longer, which in the end is what decides how long the console you already own remains worth it.
