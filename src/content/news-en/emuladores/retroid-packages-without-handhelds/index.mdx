Over the past few weeks, reports have multiplied from users who open their **Retroid Pocket Nova** package and find a sealed but empty box. It is not that the shipment never arrives: what is missing is the console. According to those affected, someone would have taken the device and resealed the packaging before delivery.

The cases have been gathering in several threads on the Retroid subreddit, along with an open thread meant to collect documented instances. It is worth stressing: these are customer reports, not an official confirmation, and so far neither any authority nor the brand itself has pointed to those responsible.

## The Retroid Pocket Nova consoles that arrive empty

The most repeated pattern points to shipments handled by **YunExpress** that pass through Chicago. Several users link those routes to **GOFO**, a third-party delivery service whose operation differs from Amazon or the United States Postal Service.

In one case, a customer shared the photograph GOFO sent as proof of delivery. The image shows that the first layer of tape had been cut and a second layer applied over it. Today, that detail is the graphic evidence most cited by those affected.

## Water damage, a second front

On top of the thefts comes a less flashy problem: there are reports of packages arriving wet. In those cases the device is still inside the box, but it does not always come out unscathed. One user described that the buttons on their unit did not respond, while another said their Classic appeared undamaged, though they did not trust it to work without issues later on.

There is still no explanation of why they arrive damp, nor data to estimate how many shipments might be affected between thefts and damage. It is not the first time the brand has made headlines this year: a few weeks ago it emerged that [the Retroid Pocket 5 and Pocket Flip 2 hid a 120 Hz screen their software did not enable](/noticias/retroid-pocket-upgrade-sorpresa/).

## No answers until October 8

Part of the frustration comes from the calendar. Retroid and other companies based in China are on holiday for the national celebration, a pause that began last week and runs until **October 8**. For those affected, that means answers will take at least those days.

Retro Handhelds, the outlet that gathered the cases, contacted its Retroid representative and received a terse reply: the matter will be forwarded to the logistics contractor "when we are back from the National Day holiday (8/10)". Until then, the company has given no public explanation.

## What affected buyers can do

The first step, those affected agree, is to **open a claim with Retroid**, if only to put the problem on their record. In addition, depending on how the purchase was paid, there is room to seek a refund: **PayPal**, the card-issuing bank or the credit company have their own dispute mechanisms.

The situation remains open and the original outlet has said it will keep tracking it. For now, the wait lasts until the end of the Chinese holiday to see whether the brand and its shipping contractor offer an explanation and a solution to customers who paid for a console and received an empty box.
